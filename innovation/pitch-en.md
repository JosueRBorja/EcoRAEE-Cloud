# AWStore — presentation script

Suggested structure for a five-minute presentation, including the demonstration. Rehearse and adjust timing; this is a script, not evidence of a presentation already delivered.

## The problem — approximately 45 seconds

When a used laptop reaches a collection point, its story often becomes fragmented. One person records its arrival, another writes the diagnosis, and a third decides what happens to its components. If those records are disconnected, we cannot reliably explain where the equipment came from, who evaluated it, or whether its final destination was appropriate.

AWStore is our fictional technology business and circular-economy case study. It combines a technology shop with a traceability platform for used electronic equipment. Our goal is to connect commercial operations with responsible recovery, while keeping clear evidence of what the system can and cannot demonstrate.

## The solution — approximately 60 seconds

Customers can browse computers, audio products, peripherals and components. They can create an account, place a demonstration order and request collection of used equipment. Staff use the same sign-in form. After authentication, the server reads the account's assigned role and opens the appropriate workspace.

Reception registers individual devices or batches. Technicians evaluate their components. Inventory staff record compatible destinations and coordinate transfers between centers. A transfer cannot exceed the available capacity, and its receipt requires evidence. Related changes are committed together, so a failed operation does not leave half of the movement recorded.

Photos are optional. A person can use the platform without uploading a profile picture, and products without a photo receive an illustration. Profile pictures are private; commercial photos have a different access policy. This is a practical example of translating a privacy and inclusion concern into a product decision.

## Demonstration — approximately 90 seconds

Show the public catalog and its technology categories. Sign in with a synthetic staff account and demonstrate the assigned workspace. Open a received device, its diagnosis and its evidence. Show the destination restrictions for a non-recoverable component.

Next, open the circular network. Select a source, a sink and a month. Run the capacity analysis and explain the maximum flow and the minimum cut. Compare the current scenario with an expanded-capacity scenario. Explain that a simulation does not change the operational capacity or authorize a real transfer.

## Integration and responsible innovation — approximately 60 seconds

The project connects the five subjects. Discrete mathematics supplies formal rules and the directed-capacity model. Database management supplies keys, constraints, views, transactions, indexes and recovery. Enterprise architecture connects the business problem, processes, information and roadmap. Responsible innovation examines who benefits, who may be excluded, and how privacy and environmental claims should be handled. Cloud architecture defines a later deployment stage, which has not been executed in this local version.

The maximum-flow model helps identify capacity bottlenecks. It does not optimize driving routes, establish environmental certification or decide who deserves a recovered device. Those decisions require additional criteria and human accountability.

## Evidence and next steps — approximately 45 seconds

Our evidence includes automated local tests, a manually checkable network, capacity scenarios, an index comparison and a backup-and-restore check. We distinguish observed technical results from proposed social benefits. We do not claim reduced emissions without measurements.

The current version runs locally with synthetic data and demonstration payments. Before public operation, we need the authorized cloud deployment, an appropriate production database and object storage, operational identities, HTTPS, monitoring, cost controls and further usability and security review. Our commitment is to make each claim traceable to an actual requirement, test and result.
